## P747 - the card drag: why three fixes did not land

Denis reported the same thing four times and the fourth report carried the
diagnosis: *"I see the card scaling up as I drag it, but when it turns grey
in one frame it scales back down to base size."*

Glow and scale vanishing **together** is not a logic bug. They lived in one
declaration block keyed on `var(--arm)`, and a custom property with no
fallback is invalid at computed-value time when unset - at which point the
browser discards the **entire declaration**, filter and scale both. So any
frame where `--arm` was not yet written (the class lands before the first
value is set) rendered as base size with no glow, which is exactly what a
one-frame switch to grey looks like.

Every `var(--arm)` now carries `,0`. The probe deliberately removes the
property mid-drag: the card holds scale 1 and keeps its glow.

**Lesson for the instrument**: the ramp probe passed three times while the
game failed, because it only ever sampled frames where `--arm` was set. A
probe that samples the states the code *intends* cannot see a state the
code falls into. Sample the absent case too.

The refusal text now goes through `setStatusMsg(why,'red')` - the same strip
every card announce uses, so it is positioned and scaled like the rest of
the table's voice by construction. `#famWhyNot` and its CSS are retired.

## P748 - four attempts on the card glow, and why only the fourth could work

P727 made it bigger. P746 gave it a floor. P747 fixed a real CSS
invalidation bug. None of them could have worked, because all three were
adjusting a mechanism that never paints on the target platform.

**The glow was a `filter: drop-shadow()` on the card.** `#famRowP` carries
`perspective(900px) rotateX(-9deg)`, so a card in hand lives inside a
3D-transformed composited context, and WebKit clips such an element's
filter to its own box. `saturate()`/`brightness()` are per-pixel and
in-bounds, so the grey ramp survives. A halo is outside the box by
definition, so it is clipped to nothing. `scale` is not a filter, so the
lift works. Denis's three symptoms - scales up, greys gradually, no glow -
are that one fact, and he named it himself: "no glow layer, nothing."

**This file already knew.** Line ~20453: *"iOS WebKit ignores filters on a
parent of 3D-transformed children"*, written when the DICE were moved off
filters for exactly this. The knowledge existed and did not transfer to
the cards, because nothing made it a shared rule - it was a comment beside
one fix.

The fix is one painter: `_paintHalo` lifted verbatim out of `_drawGlow`,
called by the dice and by `cardGlow`, painting on a screen-space canvas.
The iOS stroke-ring fallback that makes the selection glow work on his
phone now carries the card glow too, for free.

### Instrument notes (three failures in one session, all mine)

1. `getComputedStyle` said "glow present" three times. It reports what the
   cascade RESOLVED. It cannot see clipping, compositing, or paint.
2. The pixel reader returned a confident `NO GLOW` off coordinates that
   mixed two systems - the card's rect inside the lab's iframe added to
   that iframe's on-screen offset, while the lab renders it CSS-scaled to
   ~0.43x. The tell was there: **identical readings at every distance**,
   which is flat background, not a falloff. A uniform series is not a
   measurement.
3. The first real-game run reported `cardGlow: missing` - and the filter
   string beside it said `saturate(0.18) brightness(0.5)`, the *fcv-cant*
   branch. My rigged pair never satisfied honeytrap, so it measured the
   unplayable path, where absence of a glow is correct. Probes now assert
   `_famWhyNot()===''` and refuse to return a verdict otherwise.

And Denis's correction - "your screenshot is from the lab by the way not
the game" - is the reason the final numbers come from `fark_proto.html`
top-level via `?vagatest=1`, with no iframe anywhere.

## P750 - the card was being deleted while it was dragged

Five reports of "no glow". The glow was never the problem.

`famRenderRow()` replaces `hostP.innerHTML` wholesale and it runs **while
a drag is in flight**. The card being held becomes a detached node:
`_famDrag.el` animates something no longer on screen, and what the player
actually sees is a fresh element at rest with no `fcv-drag` and no
`--arm`. Measured: the dragged card read `isConnected` **true** before the
gesture and **false** after, with the glow registry emptied by its own
`isConnected` guard.

That one cause produced every symptom, across four attempts:

| Denis said | What it actually was |
|---|---|
| "it scales back down to base size" | fresh element, at rest |
| "the grey switches in one frame" | the old node's ramp abandoned |
| "no glow at all" | halo entry dropped, element gone |

P727 (bigger), P746 (floor), P747 (a real but secondary `var(--arm)`
invalidation) and P748 (moved onto the shared painter) were all aimed at
the look. None could have worked. The row now defers while a hand is
closed on a card and rebuilds once at the end of the gesture.

**Why five rounds.** Every instrument I built sampled *state* - computed
style, then canvas pixels - and none sampled *identity*. `isConnected`
before/after settled it in a single run. `feedback_log_identity_not_state`
already said this, in these words, about the dice dim system. Second time.

Also fixed here: P749 makes every drag light (colour is the verdict -
gold fires, red refuses) because P748 drew nothing for an unplayable
card, which is precisely the drag Denis kept testing; and the card
painter no longer borrows `D3X.GLOW.line`, which is the dice's own dial.

## 2026-08-16 (evening): P755 - the seam was the normal mask

Denis: "you can see on your own screenshot the seam lines" - and he was
right; the zoom settled it in one look after TWO plausible theories died
by measurement (multi-mesh span mismatch: the die is one mesh, span 1;
world-scale clamp crease: world scale is 1). The line crosses the TOP
face - the face the mask excludes - because the exclusion keyed on
per-pixel NORMALS and the authored die is softly bevelled: the top-face
normals spread enough that the smoothstep crossed its threshold
mid-face. Wherever a normal threshold lands on a hand-modelled mesh, it
draws a line. Widening the band (P753) only moved it.

The mask keys on HEIGHT now: a settled die's scoring face is its highest
region, and world height is C0-continuous over the mesh - the relight
fades out 0.15..0.42 die-heights above centre and there is no crease
anywhere for a threshold to sit on. The normal varying is deleted.

Card halo: a thin UNDER-glow (dyF drop, swept punch so no detached band,
reach 6 / rim 2.5) - silhouette intact, badge clear.

Rule worth keeping: on authored meshes, never gate a shading term on a
per-pixel normal threshold; gate on continuous quantities (position,
height, screen ramp) and the term cannot manufacture edges.
