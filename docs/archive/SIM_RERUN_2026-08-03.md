# Sim re-run — the tier ladder, 2026-08-03

**Deltas only. Nothing has been tuned against these, and nothing should be
until you've read them.** The previous results are archived as
`archive/SIM_RESULTS_2026-07-31.md` and were stale by their own admission —
they predate the zero-point sweep removal, the Trade harness fix and the
2026-08-02 rulings.

**Before any of it ran, the harness had to be fixed.** P427/P428 renamed the
table-rule ids and the harness still held the old map — so `GEAR.night8` asked
for Kindred and would have run **bare**, and anything asking for Zero Hour would
have got **Grog's Last Call**, a live bank-void rule, measured under the wrong
name. Both silent. Fixed in P437 before a single batch ran.

## Method

`tools/sim_tier_sweep.js` — four roster agents (`greg_naive`, `greg_informed`,
`carl`, `randy`), N=220 each, so **880 matches per tier**. Un-upgraded build
(`GEAR.night1`: six bone dice, no enchants, no badge, no cards), because that is
the control the original finding used. Seed varies per tier, fixed per agent.

**This is a control, not the player experience.** Nobody reaches tier 7 on six
bone dice. It measures how the LADDER scales, with player power held still.

## Three deltas that matter

**1. Win rate collapsed roughly four-fold.** Tiers 3–7 were `30.8 / 33.0 / 36.4
/ 33.9 / 32.3`. They are now `8.1 / 8.0 / 8.9 / 11.1 / 8.2`. The old claim was
"difficulty is flat from tier 3 to 7" — it is still flat across those tiers, but
at **a quarter of the level**, and the drop from tier 0 (20.1%) to tier 3 (8.1%)
is now the sharpest move on the ladder.

**2. Cap endings start far earlier than reported.** The finding said 0.3% at
tier 3 rising to 85.5% at tier 7. Measured now: **55.4% at tier 3**, 87.0% at
tier 7. So the clock is deciding the majority of matches from the middle of the
run, not just at the end of it.

**3. And here is the mechanism, which the old table did not show.** Median turns
**pins to the cap (8) from tier 3 onward and never moves again**, while:

- **player bank plateaus** — 1,971 → 1,965 → 2,030 → 2,117 → 1,933. It does not
  grow across five tiers.
- **opponent bank keeps climbing** — 5,727 → 6,436.

An un-upgraded player scores the same from tier 3 to tier 7 while the opposition
does not. The ladder scales; the control does not. That is the whole gap, and it
says the pressure is on **targets and opponent scaling**, not on how long a
match runs.

**4. And the sharpest evidence in the table is the one easiest to file as a
footnote: agent spread narrows as tiers rise** — 60.9 at tier 0 down to 23.6 at
tier 7.

A ladder that genuinely tests skill should reward better policies **more** as it
gets harder, not less. The gap between the best and worst agent doing the
opposite is the inverse of what a difficulty curve is supposed to produce. The
4x win-rate collapse is the bigger number, but this is the one that says the
ladder has stopped being a test: when every policy converges on the same low
result, the outcome is being decided by the tier, not by the player.

## What this does NOT say

- **It is not the difficulty a real player feels.** Held-still gear is the
  point of a control and the limit of it. A tier-7 run with real dice, enchants
  and cards is a different measurement and has not been made.
- **880 matches per tier**, four agents. Enough to see a four-fold move; not
  enough to argue about one or two points.
- **It does not tell you which lever to pull.** Raising NPC aggression, lowering
  late targets, and letting player scoring grow are all consistent with this
  table. The master brief's own instruction — *"tune TARGETS down before
  inflating player scoring"* — is unchanged by it.

## Reproduce

```bash
node tools/sim_run.js tools/sim_tier_sweep.js
```

~37 seconds. An earlier attempt at eight agents × N=600 ran past ten minutes;
the narrowed spread answers the same question at a quarter of the cost.
