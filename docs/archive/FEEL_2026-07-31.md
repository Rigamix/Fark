## Read this before anything below it

**Nobody played the game.** Everything here is read off source code and off a
simulation harness. No one drove the live interface, watched a match, or heard
it. That is a real limit on a document about *feel*, and it is stated first
rather than buried, because several findings would be settled in ninety seconds
at the actual table.

**The five-minute checks that need eyes, not numbers:**

1. Break an Amber die and keep rolling. Does the turn ever end? (§2)
2. Tap all six dice with one brand showing and press ROLL. This is the last gap
   between the harness's copy of the accept rule and the shipped one.
3. Bust, and time it. The brief says under 600ms.
4. Take one screenshot mid-match and answer six questions from it: your total,
   their total, the target, turns left, current selection value, turn bank.
5. Turn the sound on first — see §7.

**And the numbers have a shelf life.** Every win-rate figure below was measured
*before* the zero-point sweep was removed, and the sweep alone was worth +80.5
points. Directions are safe; magnitudes are not. Re-measure before tuning
against any specific number.

## What already works — protect this

A plan that fixes the problems below and breaks any of these is a net loss.

- **The escalation lands.** Night 8 does not feel like night 1: paired, n=800,
  tier 3 goes 30.8% → 81.5%, tier 0 goes 63.4% → 96.8%. Bosses: Grog 51.9 →
  90.7, Ambrose 30.5 → 78.3. The power fantasy is real and measured.
- **Dice carry it on their own.** Night-1 → night-8 *dice only*, no brand, no
  badge, no card: 36.4% → 94.2%. Family cards add +29.2 on top. Whatever
  happens to brands, do not weaken family dice to compensate.
- **The build runs actually reach already delivers.** Six family dice, two
  brands, a badge, three cards — the realistic night-8 loadout — is +64.0.
  If the spec gets cut to what a run can afford, the arc still lands.
- **Honest playstyles are genuinely equal.** On identical gear the humanlike
  agents spread 4.1 points, and the two most different bank policies differ by
  0.1. That is the brief's intent, and it is a standing ruling. Do not add
  banking-execution punishments to widen the skill gap.
- **The Break timing read is the one place the rework's intended skill
  measurably works.** "Hold the skull until no future turn remains" is worth
  +22.3 on a Break build — and **exactly 0.0 on an unbranded control, with zero
  discordant pairs.** That is precisely how a skill should behave. Any Break
  redesign must keep the last-turn read paying.
- **The correctness layer is solid.** 96,000 branded-face draws with zero
  illegal faces; forged 2/3/4/6 refused with 0g charged; the one-Ward cap holds
  through eight purchase routes; Trade restores bit-for-bit across 123 fires;
  a broken die returns next match with material, brands and faces intact.
- **The seven Break rows are legible** — driven individually, each fires its own
  verb and only its own. Jade never paid Obsidian's 1000.
- **The sweep fix, Vagabond's steal, the overtime terminator, the turn cap.**
  All landed, all verified. §8 lists them.

## 1. The game promises things it never does

*Verified in code. Doesn't move when the sim is re-run. Cheapest fixes in the
document — which is why it is first.*

**What it feels like:** you are shown a reward and given nothing, with no
message telling you why. Every instance fails the same way — silently.

| Promise | What actually happens |
|---|---|
| **Corvus's Ledger** — "Every bank +300, and +5g besides" | Pays the 5g. Nothing in the file dispatches on its `starstone_bonus` mechanic string (`fark_proto.html:11811`); the only bank-time code that could pay it is a hardcoded `m==="starstone"` filter the relic doesn't match. |
| **Preserve** — a die you can look at, sitting in amber | Points only. The entire file contains one occurrence of `_preserved` (`:17643`) — the guard itself. `G._famPreserve` is `{val,pts,crack}` (`:13310`): no die, no lane. The rule that a preserved die can't be Broken guards a state that cannot exist. |
| **Renown** — accumulates all run, ticks you from nobody to a Name | Every mechanical perk it reads is wiped on every save touch: `S.renownPerks={}` at `:9307`, comment "perk ladder deleted". The title still ticks. |
| **BOOKKEEPER feat** — "Bookends pays three times in one match" | Bookends was collapsed into Vanguard. Unreachable. |
| **Buying a die** | Silently deletes the brand on that slot. No refund, no warning, no confirm. |

**Levers:** wire the Ledger to whatever Starstone becomes; refund the brand on
die purchase; delete or hide the dead renown surface and the BOOKKEEPER feat.
Preserve is the one real decision here — it is built, 15 of 16 routes pass, and
it is simply never applied. Applying it costs art plus a targeting step, since
the ruling says the player picks which die goes in the amber.

## 2. The game sells you ways to stop playing it

*Three exhibits, one consequence. Rated highest because each one individually
removes the reason to press ROLL.*

Push-your-luck is the whole game, and the master brief bans removing risk by
name: *"Pure passive advantage is banned"* — an effect that removes risk without
adding one does not ship.

**Exhibit A — Amber: the turn that never has to end.** Break an Amber die and
the turn cannot bust. If the dice come up dead the game silently re-rolls them
until they aren't (`doBust`, `:24428`, a `while(_amTries++<40)` loop), and there
is no per-turn roll cap anywhere in the game. Asked for 20,000 points, **98.5%
of immune turns were still running** when the harness's own 60-roll guard
stopped them. One turn-1 Break takes a near-starting build from 35.7% → 98.7%.
*The brief says this on purpose:* "bust-immune for its remainder". Fixing it
means reversing a written ruling, not fixing a bug.

**Exhibit B — Fair Trade into Break: the die that costs nothing.** Borrow a die,
break the borrowed one, collect the guaranteed family payout, still have six
dice. `_removeDieAt`'s Fair-Trade branch (`:17696`) hands the lane back and
returns early — the splice and the `numDice` decrement below it never run.
Borrow-and-break **48.6%** at 6.00 dice; break-your-own **15.2%** at 5.00.
*This matches ruling #2 exactly.* Again, the ruling is the hole.

**Together, on six free bone dice:** 6 bone + a stashed Amber + Fair Trade + one
Break wins **500 of 500** patron matches, 6,983 points a turn, 1.02 turns a
match — against 99.7% versus a tier-3 boss. Its own control, same build without
the lend-and-break, is 34.4%. A perfectly-played honest night-8 build tops out
at 3,420 a turn. **The cheapest exploit beats the most expensive honest build by
more than 2×.**

**Exhibit C — Starstone: points without rolling.** +500 per Starstone die on
*every* bank, gated on nothing but banking at least 1 point (`:25490`). It
doesn't have to score, doesn't have to be rolled. Banking a lone 5 for 50 with
three Starstone banks 1,550. **The optimal line is to bank as small and as often
as the cap allows** — the exact inverse of a push-your-luck game. Three Starstone
is +15,000 across the 10-turn boss cap, more than Ambrose's 12,500 target before
a die is rolled.

**The consequence:** two Starstone plus four bone at 1,400g wins **77.5% for a
completely random player**, against an all-bone baseline of 3.0%. Roster spread
across eight agents collapses from 34.8 points to 4.3. It stops mattering who is
holding the controller.

**Levers**
- *Amber:* (a) one bust, not every bust — it becomes a one-shot like its five
  siblings; (b) cap rolls per turn — also caps every future runaway, but adds a
  rule to teach; (c) end immunity at N rolls or N points. **(a) recommended.**
- *Fair Trade:* (a) Break refuses a borrowed die — clean, reverses ruling #2;
  (b) breaking the loan costs the lane for the match; (c) leave it and re-price
  Fair Trade as the premium way to buy a Break trigger. **(a) recommended.**
- *Starstone:* (a) gate it on the die being kept and scored this bank — makes it
  a die trait instead of an income stream, and folds it under Still Waters;
  (b) per-match cap or diminishing stack; (c) threshold bonus, so it rewards
  pushing rather than nibbling; (d) just cut the number — but a flat number is
  what the brief bans, so this trades one violation for a smaller one.
  **(a) or (c) recommended.**

⚠️ **Starstone currently has no owner.** It is blocker #3 of five in the sim
report, it is in none of the six items in `DECISIONS_NEEDED_2026-07-31.md`, and
no commit claims it. It fell out of both lists. That is my error.

## 3. You can't tell what just happened

*Not measured by the sim at all — found by reading the feedback path. This is
the most feel-shaped problem in the document.*

**The whole game speaks through one line of text.** Every announcement — brands
firing, Ward, Amber, Tithe, Break, card effects, shop purchases, refunds — goes
through `famLog` (`:12287`), a one-line wrapper around `setStatusMsg`. It holds
**one message at a time**, and it lives **inside the match screen**.

Two consequences, both invisible:

- **Two things firing together means one of them was never announced.** It gets
  overwritten before anyone reads it.
- **Anything firing in the shop, the loadout or the night screen is announced
  into a hidden div.** The message is generated correctly and displayed nowhere.

This is why "the player can't tell why they lost" keeps appearing under other
headings. The brief's rule is *every rule that changes a number shows its change
at the number it changes* — the mechanism to do that doesn't exist yet.

**Related, same shape:** the in-game rules screen is the only teaching surface
in the build, and it teaches rules the code doesn't implement. `:8582` tells the
player *"Losing to a patron costs nothing."* It does — seats per night are
finite, so a loss spends one. Patron gold pays three to four times what the
screen says. **A player who reads the manual comes away worse informed.**

**And nothing in the shop asks "are you sure."** A 400g brand is bought by
tapping a die. A 700g die is bought by dropping it — and that drop silently
deletes whatever brand was on the slot. The one screen that says a brand is
permanent sits on a code path the player can never reach.

**Levers:** a message queue rather than a single slot; a feedback surface that
exists outside the match screen; a confirm step on irreversible purchases;
an audit pass on the rules screen against current behaviour. None of these are
design decisions — they're all "the thing the brief already asks for".

## 4. Brands: Break is the whole cost, and the one real skill lives there

*Downgraded from the sim's framing. The headline number does not survive its own
caveats — but the narrow finding is solid.*

The briefed six-brand loadout wins **18 points less** than the identical build
with no brands. But the evidence disqualifies the wide reading four ways: two
readers disagree by an order of magnitude on what non-Break brands cost; the sim
document itself says *"Do not price the brands off either number until a single
crossed design settles it"*; everything was measured before the sweep was
removed; and the harness could not put a Trade brand on a die at all (fixed
today — see §8).

**What survives, and it is narrow: Break alone is the cliff.** −29.0 of the
−18.0 total. And playing it correctly returns +22.3 of that — while being worth
exactly 0.0 on an unbranded control. So the rework **did** create a real skill;
it just priced the tuition higher than the degree.

The felt problem is the shape, not the size: brands are the only purchase in the
shop that is *skill-gated to break even* while everything else is an
unconditional gain. A player who doesn't know the read is punished for engaging
with the system the whole rework exists for.

**Levers:** (a) retune Break's cost so a naive player is roughly neutral and a
good one is ahead — it's the one row carrying the loss; (b) buff the other five
and leave Break as the risky one; (c) accept it as a deliberate expert layer and
make the tooltip say so. **(a) recommended**, then re-measure the rest.

## 5. You're saving toward a build the game never pays for

Maxed loadout: **5,990g**. A perfect run earns **5,921g** lifetime and **4,205g**
before night 8. *(Second reader computed 6,913g from the constants — nobody
adjudicated. Either way it's at or below the briefed cost, but the margin is the
difference between "barely affordable" and "impossible", and it should be
settled before pricing.)*

Compounding it: **buying a die destroys that slot's brand with no refund**, so
the correct purchase order is dice-then-brands — which is exactly the order that
guarantees you never reach brands. In practice a dedicated shopper reaches
**2.0 of 6**; jade2 is bought in 2% of runs. Shop scarcity adds noise on top:
each family die is stocked on 55% of nights and sold out on 45%
(`_shopRollNight`, `:10614`), with at most four stands displayed.

**Levers:** (a) refund the brand when its die is replaced — fixes the ordering
trap on its own and is the fair reading of what the player bought;
(b) raise payouts so the curve clears ~6,000g; (c) cut brand prices.
**(a) is worth doing regardless of the pricing answer.**

## 6. The late game stops being a race and becomes arithmetic

Night-1 win rate at tiers 3–7: **30.8 / 33.0 / 36.4 / 33.9 / 32.3** — flat inside
its own error bars. Tiers 4–7 aren't harder, they're **longer**.

What actually changes is the shape. Patron targets climb 5,000 → 9,500 while the
opponent's mean bank barely moves (4,473 → 6,220), so **cap-decided endings go
0.3% at tier 0 to 85.5% at tier 7.** Most late matches stop being a race to the
line and end on a scoreboard comparison. The big number on the HUD becomes
set-dressing.

The master brief's own instruction for this case is *"Tune TARGETS down before
inflating player scoring."*

**Levers:** (a) raise NPC bank aggression with tier instead of raising targets —
both sides climb, and the race feel comes back; (b) lower late targets so the
line is reachable inside 8 turns, which is what the brief instructs; (c) shorten
the late cap so a cap ending arrives sooner and reads as intended; (d) make the
cap ending legible — highest-tally is a real rule and the HUD doesn't sell it.
**(a) recommended**, (d) regardless.

## 7. Still Waters punishes exactly the investment it should protect

*Narrowed from "badges". Half the badge evidence is a hole in the harness — four
of eight were never exercised, and their 0.0 is an absence of a test, not a
verdict. This one stands on code, not simulation.*

`_famHushed` (`:17542`) is `!!(d && d.ench && _stillWaters())`. **The `d.ench`
test means an unbranded die is never hushed.** So the badge silences only the
dice you paid to brand, while a plain die keeps its family trait in full. It
costs **−23.6** on a branded build.

It also fails as the counter the brief says it is: Break needs only **one**
branded die anywhere, and the die it destroys is unbranded and therefore
unhushed. The cheapest build — Break on one die, plain Obsidian everywhere else
— walks straight through it with the badge worn. Measured: 200 driven breaks,
all-branded → 1000 without badge / 0 with; **one-branded → 1000 both ways.**

**Levers:** (a) hush by family rather than by brand — makes the brief's claim
true and stops it punishing investment; (b) let it reach the brand itself, or at
least the Break payout. **(a) recommended** — but note `AUDIT_RESOLUTIONS` #5
already ruled family-suppression while `DECISIONS_NEEDED` #5 re-opens it as an
unmade choice. One of those documents has to win (see §9).

the FOUNDATIONAL document, not the final word, and five later documents win on
any topic they cover. It also says plainly that the line-by-line pass to strike
the stale text this implies is NOT done, and flags it as owed work rather than

DECISIONS_NEEDED open on purpose.

The contradictions below are kept as the record of what that block resolves.
Still Waters and the cursed seat are settled; the master brief's stale enchant

badge sections. `FARK_MATCH_BRIEF` says "where this conflicts with older

The live consequences:

- **The master brief's whole enchant menu and Silver identity no longer exist** —
  it still sells "Silver, 580g: saves you from one bust per match" and still

## 10. What nobody measured — so nobody reads a hole as a zero

- **Four of eight badges never exercised** (Steeped, Pickpocket, Drill Order,
  In Arrears/First Strike). Their 0.0 is an absence of a test.
- **Quicksilver never driven.** Snuff, Fog and Snare's real bite on the opponent
  is unmeasured. The Fair Trade *card* was never played.
- **Break was never offered in the shared shop** — no agent ever drafted the most
  consequential brand in the game.
- **The shop, the gold award and the card draft were modelled, not driven.**
- **Median banked turns per side** — an acceptance target — has no measured
  median from anyone.
- **Whether gold can be farmed by abandoning matches** was never tested, and
  Tithe writes `S.run.gold` and saves mid-match.
- **The run-level win rate is unknown** — the 52% figure comes from a loop that
  lets you re-challenge a boss.
- **Silver's bust ratio is ruled as ~0.55× bone and stable.** A wider sweep found
  **0.126 to 0.864** across 17 policy cells, and 0.234 in matches actually
  played — roughly twice as protective as the ruling says. The stability claim
  does not survive.

## 11. The moments that should land hardest are the least finished

- **The game opens silent.** Music, ambience and SFX are all forced off on first
  touch behind a one-time `_protoMuted` flag ("proto build: audio off by default
  (Denis)"). Every commit ding, bank flourish and bust thud is invisible to
  anyone playing a fresh install — **including anyone assessing how this feels.**
  One line to flip, and it changes what every subsequent feel assessment is
  assessing.
- **The victory screen still reads "LAST ORDERS RUNG"** — wrong under either
  meaning now that Last Orders is the night-end beat. The correct headline was
  already ruled; this is the build catching up, not an open question.
- **The victory ending still lands on the greybox placeholder**, and the creative
  director ruled it must not share the loss screen. Real art cost.
