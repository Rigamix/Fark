## 1. Which option — A, B, C, or D

**Option A.**

Rejecting the other three plainly, not just picking A by default:
- **B** (a roll-floor on Zero Hour) dilutes the badge roster — two of eight
  badges become roll-limiters instead of one — and bundles two different
  lessons into a single rule, which is more for a player to learn, not less.
- **C** (leave it inert) is indefensible given Grog's position is fixed as the
  first fight. The game's most-played, most-formative encounter would carry a
  rule that can never do anything there.
- **D** (give bosses brands early, including Grog) breaks the progression
  curve every other system in this game respects, solely to make one badge's
  math work at a position it doesn't belong.

One correction to the doc's own framing before the sub-answers: the "three-way
reshuffle with Corvus" reasoning is now stale. Corvus's two problems (First
Strike's downgrade, In Arrears' orphaned gold-tax) were already resolved
together in the prior round — one badge, both halves of his identity restored.
This is a two-way reshuffle, not three.

## 2. If A: what does Grog get, and who inherits Zero Hour

**Grog gets a retuned Last Call, not a new concept.** It already taught the
right lesson (bank-threshold awareness, legible on plain dice, no prior
game-knowledge required) and only tested weak on magnitude, not on the idea
itself. Restoring his original identity with corrected numbers is lower-risk
than inventing something new for the game's first impression.

**Zero Hour moves to whichever boss holds the LAST slot before Ambrose —
tied to POSITION, not to a fixed name.** This is deliberate: it keeps the
assignment robust against the boss-reordering idea raised separately (still
just a proposal, not built) — if that ships, Zero Hour doesn't need a third
reshuffle to follow whichever boss ends up in the late slot. Ambrose himself
stays untouched, consistent with his existing protected status as the
run's climactic escalation.

## 3. Opponent-side enchant scheduling

**After the pricing pass, not scheduled now.** It's real feature work — an AI
keep-policy for icon faces, not a quick patch — and nothing is currently
broken without it. First Strike and Kindred both function correctly in their
already-shipped rescoped forms. Landing the smaller, already-queued fixes
(pricing, the five unvalidated Break rows, Preserve) on a stable foundation
first is worth more than pulling a larger feature ahead of them.
